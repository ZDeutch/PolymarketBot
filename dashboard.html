How It Works
🔗

Data Pipeline

1
Lichess Broadcast API — live round results as games finish
2
FIDE / Chess.com ratings — current Elo with Chess.com fallback
3
TPR database — 650+ players, 662 elite games across 7 tournaments
4
Market prices — Polymarket CLOB API or Kalshi (hardcoded)
5
Edge table + CSV log — actionable bets written to positions.csv
🎲

Monte Carlo Engine

  • 50,000 tournament simulations per run, full round-robin schedule
  • Elo win probability with +35 white-piece advantage baked in
  • Kryukov draw-rate model — exponential fit, optimized for the 2700+ cohort where draws dominate
  • TPR blending — 45% FIDE Elo + 55% tournament performance rating, scaled by games played
  • Rating variance — N(μ, σ=50) per simulation to capture uncertainty
  • Must-win adjustment — standings-aware draw-rate reduction for desperate positions
📐

Edge & Position Sizing

  • Edge = Model prob − Market price — positive means YES is cheap, negative means YES is overpriced
  • 8% minimum edge — 3-point buffer above 5% EV floor to absorb model error
  • Quarter-Kelly sizing — conservative fractional Kelly, caps each trade at a fraction of bankroll
  • 5% tournament exposure cap — total stake per tournament ≤ $500 on a $10K bankroll
  • Min $100 stake — ignores tiny signals not worth the operational friction

Kryukov Draw-Rate Model
Calibrated to 2700+ classical games, captures elite draw surge
▼
draw% = −|ΔR| / 32.49
        + exp((μ − 2254.7) / 208.49)
        + 23.87

// μ = average rating of the two players
// ΔR = rating difference (larger gap → fewer draws)
// Exponential term captures 2700+ draw rate explosion

At the top level, games between equally matched 2800+ players draw over 60% of the time. The exponential term in this model captures that phenomenon — draw rates grow rapidly as average rating crosses 2600 and explode above 2750.
